# Animation options — research and design proposals

25 October 2026. None of these additions is implemented. The user requested options for Madarao and Myoko; they are not recorded team decisions or audience-test findings.

## Madarao

**1. A small rabbit inspired by Furusato — recommended character option.** A brown-grey rabbit grazes, turns an ear, makes two or three short hops and settles in a forest-edge clearing. Keep it off fields, paths and houses. Nakano City's planning documents explicitly connect the rural foothill landscape below Madarao to the song *Furusato*, and local tourism material connects that landscape to lyricist Tatsuyuki Takano. The rabbit would be an artistic reference to the song's rural imagery, rather than a claim that Madarao has an established rabbit-guardian legend. It complements the fox and snake without adding another large magical figure. Avoid autoplay music.

Sources: [Nakano City master-plan summary, Toyota area](https://www.city.nakano.nagano.jp/docs/2021121300037/file_contents/gaiyou.pdf); [local operator's Madarao/Furusato route description](https://www.madaraomountainresort.com/260852641235486122881249912483124641248612531mtb124671254012473.html).

**2. A walking pair and a quiet forest breeze — recommended restrained option.** Two tiny walkers follow a short verified woodland trail segment, pause at a view and return; a few leaves move nearby. Madarao Tourism presents walking, Lake Nozomi and Numanohara Wetlands as local attractions. This conveys the region's walking culture and green-season identity; it is a contemporary place-based scene, not folklore. It is less distinctive as an easter egg than the rabbit, but directly supports the itinerary proposition. Keep movement local and secondary to the existing activity models.

Source: [Madarao Tourism Association](https://www.madarao.info/en).

## Myoko

**3. Soft onsen steam — recommended overall.** A small, translucent plume rises, spreads and dissolves at the onsen settlement on the lower mountain, rather than venting from the summit. It should be quieter than the existing broad mist banks and clearly associated with bathing culture. Japan's Environment Ministry identifies the diverse onsen at Myoko's foot as a defining feature; JNTO describes Tsubame's volcanic hot-spring waters. This is recognisable, fits the painted style and adds life without another roaming creature or any suggestion of an eruption. Position at an established onsen represented on this stylised map; do not invent a new bath or provider.

Sources: [Environment Ministry — national park features](https://www.env.go.jp/park/myokotogakushi/point/index.html); [JNTO — Tsubame Onsen](https://faq.japan-travel.jnto.go.jp/en/spot/1436/).

**4. A mountain pilgrim / yamabushi — strongest human cultural link.** A small traditionally dressed figure walks slowly along a short lower-mountain path and pauses near the shrine. The Environment Ministry explicitly relates Sekiyama Shrine's fire festival to Shugendo practised around Mount Myoko. An everyday walking/pause scene would be a design interpretation grounded in that history. Do not invent a specific ritual, costume detail, sacred gesture or historical person. This needs more research into local dress and practice before modelling and should remain subtle rather than theatrical.

Source: [Environment Ministry — mountain worship and Sekiyama festival](https://www.env.go.jp/park/myokotogakushi/point/index.html).

**5. Yamaotoko — folklore alternative.** A small, distant mountain-man figure might walk a few steps between rocks and stop to look across the valley. Niigata's cultural archive describes *Hokuetsu Kidan* accounts of yamaotoko in the Myoko–Kurohime–Yakeyama mountains. This is a documented regional association, not an exclusive Myoko guardian. The account describes an approximately human-height figure with reddish hair and grey skin; a giant lumbering monster would misrepresent that account. This is more unusual but potentially less welcoming than the steam/pilgrim options and would need careful visual treatment.

Source: [Niigata cultural archive — regional supernatural tales](https://n-story.jp/topic/41/).

My preferred pair is **Madarao rabbit + Myoko onsen steam**. It gives Madarao a character and Myoko a recognisable cultural landscape effect while keeping the map calm. If all four mountains should have a character, choose rabbit + a carefully researched pilgrim instead. New animations should share collision checks, background haze, muted colours and static reduced-motion poses.
